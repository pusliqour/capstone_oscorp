TASK MLP-001: VALIDATE RECOMMENDED STOCK RANGES
Status: AGREED, PLANNING ONLY. Experiments and agent execution are on hold until the user finishes the report/concerns walkthrough. No experiments have run for this task.
Question: For each store/product and 7, 14, 30, 60-day planning horizon, do the recommended lower/upper stock targets reduce missed demand without creating unnecessary inventory?

Current implementation
- ml_backend/stock_planning.py; docs/STOCK_PLANNING.md.
- Lower = ceil(horizon forecast + daily rate * (supplier lead time + safety days)).
- Upper additionally includes daily rate * review allowance.
- Defaults: 3 safety days, 7 review-allowance days. Explicit assumptions, not proven optimal settings or statistical confidence bounds.
- Current implementation tests verify calculation/publication/display, not the business quality of these thresholds.

Scope
1. First clarify horizon, supplier lead time, days until next order, actual ordering cadence and upper-band allowance. Audit possible double-counting. A range-width allowance must not be described as a change to actual ordering cadence unless that change is simulated.
2. Specify how the target band becomes a decision: at an allowed review, order up to the upper target when inventory position is below the lower target. Credit only deliveries with known arrival times. Keep supplier capacity, case packs, ordering schedule and other assumptions explicit.
3. Evaluate one chosen planning horizon at a time. The four rows are alternatives, not four simultaneous orders. Keep demand forecasts fixed when comparing buffer choices so forecast changes do not confound the policy comparison.
4. Compare the current 3/7 default with a no-extra-buffer ablation, the existing simulation replenishment policy, and a small parameter grid. Proposed safety grid: 0, 1, 3, 5, 7 days. Proposed upper-band allowance grid: 0, 3, 7, 14 days, with actual review timing fixed and reported. Freeze the final grid before evaluation; record any revision.
5. Include new products, intermittent/zero demand, stockouts, demand surges, slow/dead stock, delivery delays and missing delivery timing. Do not borrow another store's sales for a new product. Do not invent missing operational inputs.

Validation and evidence
- Use paired replay: identical customer demand, starting stock, supplier conditions and random delays for every policy. Hidden true demand is for grading only, never an input to a decision.
- Report lost units/lost-demand fraction, fill rate, excess/ending unsold stock and average on-hand inventory. State denominators and units. Report inventory value only when valuation assumptions are declared; do not invent costs to claim profit.
- Report all four horizons and results by region, demand class and delivery-time group. Include how often ranges were provisional or unavailable.
- Choose candidates on development/validation worlds and earlier dates only. Test on unseen stores at later dates after freezing settings.
- IMPORTANT: existing report's 200 test stores and seeds 1-4/20261111 have already been inspected. They are development evidence for this new task, not an untouched final test.
- Reserve NEW final-world seeds 20261201, 20261202, 20261203 before generating their results. Do not use them for selection. A lead agent records the candidate/freeze manifest before final runs.
- For 365-day worlds, choose evaluation origins with the entire next 60 days available (latest origin 304); no truncated 60-day labels. Training and validation outcome windows must finish before final evaluation begins.
- Repeat across worlds; report mean/spread and 95% intervals clustered by store. Preserve paired comparisons when resampling.
- Record baseline and candidate results even when the candidate loses. If missed sales and excess stock trade off, present the Pareto choices; do not declare a single best policy without the owner's service-level/cost preference.
